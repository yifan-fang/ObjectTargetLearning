Welcome!

In this experiment you will see two objects overlaid on top of each other in the centre of the screen. Both stay on screen for the whole trial, and both jiggle constantly, wobbling in small random directions.

At one moment, one of the two objects makes a slightly bigger movement: it drifts in one direction and then comes back. Your job is to report the direction of that movement with the arrow keys.

Before each trial, an arrow tells you which axis the movement will be along. The cued axis changes from trial to trial, so always check the arrow:

↔  =  press LEFT or RIGHT

↕  =  press UP or DOWN

This is a challenging task. The bigger movement is only slightly larger than the constant jiggle, so it does not jump out at you. If you truly did not see it, take a guess.

You will start with 20 easy trials for you to get familiar with the task. Then, you will do 30 practice trials that is the same as the main task. After that, you will do 8 blocks of the main task. Each block contains 60 trials, and you will be given feedback on your performance after each block. Please try your best to keep your responses fast and accurate. You can take short breaks between blocks, and the whole experiment will take about 60 minutes to complete.

In case you were curious, the category you most often had to report was:



On each trial, two object categories were superimposed at the centre of the screen, both jiggling, and at one moment one of them made a larger movement — the one you had to report. An arrow told you which axis that movement would be along. For most of the experiment, on 60% of trials the category was the object that made that movement, so it was the one you reported. On 20% of trials it was present but did not move. On the remaining 20% it did not appear at all. In the later blocks, a third of the trials showed new pictures of the same objects. On those trials the category was the one that moved on 20% of trials, was present but did not move on 20%, and did not appear on the remaining 60%; with the pictures you had already seen, the proportions stayed as before. Throughout, each of the 5 categories was the object that did not move on about 20% of trials.

The goal of this experiment is to understand how the frequency with which an object is the thing you need to attend to affects your performance.
